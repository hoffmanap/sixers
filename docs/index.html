The Sixers spent the offseason stacking stars — LeBron James, Jaylen Brown, Joel Embiid, Tyrese Maxey, VJ Edgecombe. Whether their games actually fit together is a spacing question before it's a talent question.

1. Where everyone wants to shoot from

Sprawlball's central finding was simple: the shots worth taking are at the rim and beyond the arc. Everything in between — the long two — is where offenses go to die efficiency-wise. So the first question for this roster isn't "how good is each scorer," it's whether their shot diets overlap.

Real 2025-26 zone splits. Hover a bar to isolate a player.

Maxey and Edgecombe are the Sprawlball-compliant guards — under 15% mid-range each, everything else split rim/three.

Embiid's mid-range share is up, not down, this season — 30.7%, his highest since 2023-24. The "modernized" version of his shot profile isn't holding steady.

Brown's mid-range rate nearly doubled year over year — 14.3% in 2024-25 to 23.7% in 2025-26. That's the single biggest shot-profile swing on the roster, and it's moving the wrong direction for a Sprawlball-style offense right as he's joining a team that needs him spacing the floor, not shrinking it.

2. Who actually has the ball

Four players on this roster have a real claim to touches. Basketball only has one ball. Pick-and-roll ball-handler frequency — how often a player's offensive possessions start with them running the action, not standing in the corner — is the most direct trace of who's actually initiating, season over season.

Share of offensive possessions spent as the PnR ball-handler, real data, four seasons.

Maxey is unambiguously the engine — consistently 28-35% of his possessions, more than double anyone else on this list, every single season.

LeBron's line is falling fast: from 20.8% in 2022-23 down to 11.7% this season — a real, measurable step back from ball-handling responsibility, not a hunch.

Embiid's number: essentially zero, every season measured. He is not, and has never been, a pick-and-roll initiator — whatever playmaking he offers the offense runs through the post or short roll, a different mechanism entirely.

3. Maxey's speed, Embiid's mobility

Player-tracking data logs distance covered and speed, game over game. Paired with actual on-court net rating for the Maxey-Embiid combination specifically, it's the most direct way to see whether Maxey's downhill pace is being unlocked or capped depending on who's sharing the floor with him.

Line: Maxey's distance run per game vs. league average. Bars: real Maxey+Embiid on-court net rating by season.

The pairing's net rating collapsed to -3.2 in 2024-25 — the exact season Embiid played only 315 shot-chart possessions worth of minutes, i.e. the exact season he was hurt. It recovered to +7.2 this year, right alongside Embiid's minutes recovering.

That correlation is the whole story: this isn't really a pace-and-space incompatibility, it's an availability problem wearing a scheme-fit costume.

4. The defensive bill comes due

Shooting fit is solvable with spacing. Defensive fit is solvable with effort and scheme — until it isn't. Defended-FG%-versus-normal is a blunt but real individual signal: negative means a player makes shooters worse than their own average; positive means the opposite.

2025-26 defended FG% minus shooter's normal FG%, five starters.

Embiid and Brown are both clearly plus defenders by this measure — Embiid at -4.1, Brown at -4.2, essentially tied for best on the roster.

LeBron is now a measurable defensive minus (+1.5) for the first time in this four-season window — the aging-curve concern isn't speculative anymore, it's in the tracking data.
